Crochet a Cat
How to Crochet a Cat: A Beginner-Friendly Guide
Crocheting your own cute cat is a fun and rewarding project, whether you're a beginner or a seasoned yarn artist. With just a few basic stitches and some creativity, you can craft an adorable feline friend to keep or give as a handmade gift. This guide walks you through the process of crocheting a simple amigurumi-style cat.
What You'll Need
Before you begin, gather the following materials:
Yarn: Choose worsted-weight yarn in your desired cat color(s).


Crochet hook: Typically size 3.5mm (E) or 4mm (G) works well with worsted yarn.


Stuffing: Polyester fiberfill for a soft, plush body.


Tapestry needle: For sewing pieces together.


Safety eyes or buttons: Optional, for a more finished look.


Stitch marker: To help keep track of your rounds.


Basic Stitches to Know
Magic ring: For starting circular pieces.


Single crochet (sc)


Increase (inc): Two single crochets in one stitch.


Decrease (dec): Combine two stitches into one.


Slip stitch (sl st): To join or finish rounds.


Step-by-Step: Crochet a Simple Cat
1. Head and Body (Worked in One Piece)
Start with a magic ring.
Round 1: 6 sc in magic ring
 Round 2: inc in each stitch around (12)
 Round 3: (sc, inc) around (18)
 Rounds 4–9: sc in each stitch (18)
 Round 10: (sc, dec) around (12)
 Rounds 11–12: sc in each stitch (12)
 Round 13: (sc, inc) around (18)
 Rounds 14–18: sc in each stitch (18)
 Round 19: (sc, dec) around (12)
 Round 20: dec around (6), fasten off, and stuff firmly.
2. Ears (Make 2)
Round 1: 4 sc in magic ring
 Round 2: (sc, inc) twice (6)
 Round 3: sc in each stitch (6), leave a tail for sewing.
3. Arms and Legs (Make 4)
Round 1: 6 sc in magic ring
 Rounds 2–4: sc in each stitch (6), stuff lightly and sew to body.
4. Tail
Chain 10, turn, sc in each chain, fasten off and sew to the back of the cat.
5. Assembly and Finishing Touches
Sew the ears to the top of the head.


Attach arms, legs, and tail in place.


Add safety eyes between rounds 5 and 6, about 4 stitches apart.


Use embroidery floss to stitch a nose and mouth.


Tips for Success
Use a stitch marker to track your rounds in the body and head.


Adjust the size by using thicker or thinner yarn and matching hook size.


Personalize with colors, stripes, or tiny accessories like a bow or collar.


Final Thoughts
Crocheting a cat is a delightful project that introduces amigurumi techniques in a manageable way. Once you've mastered the basics, you can experiment with different poses, expressions, and yarn textures to bring your crocheted cats to life.
